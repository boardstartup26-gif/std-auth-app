# How ICSE Class 10 Answers Are Marked

     by the grader rules in src/lib/prompts/examiner-prompt.ts. Keep it that way. -->

ICSE board answers are not marked on impression. For every question, CISCE publishes a marking scheme: the points, steps or elements an answer must contain, and how many marks each one carries. An examiner reads your answer against that list. Marks go to what the scheme asks for, and to nothing else.

That one fact explains most of the marks students lose without understanding why.

## The marking scheme is a checklist

A scheme turns a question into separate checkpoints. A three-mark question usually has three of them. Each checkpoint is either in your answer or it isn't, so the useful question after any attempt is not "was my answer good?" but "which checkpoint did I miss?".

Three consequences follow:

- **Correct content outside the scheme earns nothing.** An accurate fact the scheme doesn't list adds length, not marks.
- **Length is not merit.** A short answer that hits every checkpoint outscores a long one that misses one.
- **One idea counts once.** Restating the same point in new words does not earn a second mark.

## How each subject turns into marks

The checklist looks different from subject to subject.

| Subject | What carries the marks |
|---|---|
| History & Civics | One valid, distinct point per mark |
| Physics | Formula, substitution and final answer with unit, as separate steps |
| Chemistry | Balanced equations with their conditions; observations, not product names |
| Biology | Prescribed technical terms, precise locations and direct functions |
| Geography | Geographical terms, stated ranges, and reasons tied to a place |
| English Literature | Required elements: speaker, listener, setting, events, device, inference |

### Point-based subjects

In [History & Civics](/icse-class-10/history-civics), each valid point the scheme lists is worth a mark, up to the marks for the question. Write point by point, with clear separation, so each one is easy to find and credit.

### Step-marked numericals

In [Physics](/icse-class-10/physics), numericals are marked in steps. The formula, the substitution of values and the final answer with its unit each carry marks. A right answer with no working can lose the method marks, and a right method with a slip can still earn most of them.

### Mark-bearing words

Some words carry a mark on their own. A Biology answer that describes a cell as "loose" instead of **flaccid** loses the term mark even if the idea is right. A Physics law without its condition, such as "in equilibrium" in the principle of moments, is not the full statement. A Geography answer needs **leaching**, not "washing away". When the scheme uses a term, use it.

## Where correct-looking answers lose marks

These are the patterns that cost marks most often, across subjects:

1. **Fewer points than marks.** Count the marks before you write and make sure you have that many distinct points.
2. **Missing units and conditions.** A number without its unit, or a reaction without its catalyst and temperature, is incomplete.
3. **Naming instead of observing.** In Chemistry, "a white precipitate is formed" answers an observation question; naming the compound does not.
4. **Answering a nearby question.** Retelling a whole story, or explaining the general topic, instead of the specific line or event asked about.
5. **Vague wording.** "Near the intestine", "good weather", "helps the plant make food": the scheme wants the precise location, range or function.

> None of these are knowledge gaps. They are gaps between what you know and what the scheme can credit, which is why they keep repeating until someone shows you the exact point that was missed.

## How BoardEdge uses this

BoardEdge holds real ICSE Class 10 past-paper questions, each with its official CISCE marking scheme. When you submit an answer, it is marked only against that scheme: every awarded mark points to a scheme point you wrote, and every missed mark names the point you didn't. Objective questions are checked against the answer key exactly.
