# How to Practise ICSE Past Papers So Your Marks Improve

     actually does (see CLAUDE.md: practice papers, reattempt prompts). -->

Solving past papers is the most common ICSE revision advice, and it works, but only if each attempt tells you something. Writing an answer, glancing at a solution and moving on mostly practises what you already do. The marks come from finding the specific point you keep missing and fixing it.

Here is a method that does that.

## 1. Attempt before you look

Write the full answer first, the way you would in the exam hall: complete sentences, units, labelled points. Don't read the solution and then "check you would have known it". Recognising an answer is much easier than producing one, and only producing one is examined.

Time yourself on at least some attempts. A question you can answer in ten minutes but not in four is still a problem in a two-hour paper.

## 2. Check against the marking scheme, not a model answer

A model answer shows one good way to write it. The marking scheme shows **what earns the marks**. Go through the scheme point by point and tick only what your answer actually says, not what you meant.

Be strict with yourself:

- A point that is implied but not written is not there.
- The everyday word is not the technical term.
- A number without its unit is not the answer.
- A correct equation without its conditions does not earn the equation mark.

If you want to know how schemes award marks in detail, read [How ICSE Class 10 Answers Are Marked](/guides/how-icse-answers-are-marked).

## 3. Name the missed point

For every mark you didn't get, write down the exact scheme point you missed in a few words: "no unit", "said product not observation", "forgot ratooning", "speaker wrong". Over a few weeks, this list shows a pattern. Most students lose marks to three or four repeat habits, not to hundreds of different gaps.

## 4. Reattempt the same question

Come back to the questions you lost marks on after a day or two, then after about a week, and answer them again from scratch. The second attempt is where the fix becomes habit. If the same point is missed again, it goes to the top of your revision list.

## 5. Practise by section, then by paper

Early on, work chapter by chapter so you can see which chapters leak the most marks. Closer to the exam, attempt full papers in one sitting so you practise choosing questions and managing time.

| Stage | What to practise | What it shows you |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter practice | Questions from one chapter | Which chapters lose the most marks |
| Mixed sets | Short and long questions across chapters | Whether fixes hold outside their chapter |
| Full papers | A complete paper, timed | Time management and question choice |
